## Hypothesis and scope

Round 1 found the event premium positive at the mid and negative at the touch in every book, and
no selector that survived a held-out read. Round 2 asked whether the gap can be closed from any of
ten directions that round 1 and the closed list left open: decisions after the announcement (A,
C), the contract sold (B), a structural jump model (D), a combined walk-forward model (E), passive
exits (F), index options on mega-cap nights (G), other scheduled events (H), the entry day (I) and
name persistence (J). Success: a frozen rule passing its held-out read at t >= 3, interval above
zero and the matched null at the 95th percentile, with a book level positive at the touch.

## Verdict

No lane produced a book that is net positive at the touch, in any block. Two kinds of result
survive.

**Cost recovery that held out.** Selling the tightest-quoted of the three central strikes, with
the strike step's delta hedged over the event night (trial 1,819), gains +0.21% of m0 per trade
[+0.14, +0.28], t 5.7, N=5,092 on held-out 2019-24, frozen before the read. Its claim to be
confirmatory is contested: trial 1,560 had already scored on 2019-24 a 15:59 choice of the
cheaper-quoted construction, paired against H at the touch (+0.10% [+0.01, +0.17]), and trial
1,557 scored strike placement against H on the same block. The feature differs (adjacent-strike
width rather than construction), the outcome family does not. Read conservatively it is a strong
held-out replication of a known effect, which a t of 5.7 survives under any multiplicity. A
vega-matched strangle at half the implied move with spread-aware wings (trial 1,826) gains +0.55%
[+0.40, +0.71], t 7.0, but is exploratory and carries an unmeasured accounting difference (below).
Both books still lose: -1.05% and -0.66% of m0.

**One exploratory selector.** A hierarchical empirical-Bayes jump model (lane D) is calibrated in
aggregate out of sample: pooled realised over predicted move variance is 1.062 [0.977, 1.154] on
2013-18 and 1.062 [0.956, 1.174] on 2025-26. The decile check it failed on discovery (bottom decile
1.21 [1.006, 1.435], trial 1,890) was not run on either held-out block, and the slope of 0.41
(t 5.2) of model-minus-market on the market's error is in-sample, measured on the block used to
choose the model's normaliser and recalibration. Its richest tercile beat cell-matched controls on
held-out 2013-18 by +0.101 of premium [+0.037, +0.173], t 2.91, null 99.9, and failed its second
read on 2025-26 (+0.050, t 1.39). 2013-18 was not clean either: round 1's trials 1,689 to 1,691
sorted on implied-minus-forecast move richness over 2016-19, and the buy-back outcome was promoted
to primary after discovery. The conservative reading is one exploratory held-out pass below t 3
whose replication failed. The selected tercile's hedged hold is break-even on 2013-18 (-0.08% of
m0) and +2.40% [+0.92, +3.82] on 2025-26, a block viewed many times before whose hold table also
drops 91 events with no exit leg, the kind that loses. The frozen megacap replication (trial 2,081) was
not read: with 324 treated events its minimum detectable effect, 0.11 to 0.14, sits above the
effect to be replicated.

## What the round establishes

The loss sits in the event-night gap and the entry spread, and nothing decided after the
announcement recovers it. With perfect foresight, choosing hold or buy-back event by event gives
-0.13% [-0.56, +0.30] of m0 on 2013-18 and +0.10% [-0.25, +0.45] on 2019-24 (lane A, after the
netting correction below); realistic rules stay within 0.07% of always holding. The hold beats the
buy-back because it avoids the exit half-spread (+1.5% of m0), not because a post-event volatility
premium remains: that premium is +0.15% and -0.22% of m0 net of hedging (lane C).

The event premium does not transfer. Index options price the correlated mega-cap jump at about
what it delivers (implied 6.3e-5 against realised 9.3e-5 of extra night variance, lane G);
pharma advisory committees carry no premium beyond ordinary weeks (lane H); entering earlier pays
the spread on a larger premium and gives away the run-up (lane I).

Selection on observables is weak and mostly the closed spread gate. A model trained on net
outcomes rebuilds the spread gate (lane E); name-level persistence is the spread itself
(entry-spread persistence r +0.67, lane J). The jump model is the exception, because it prices the
event from a calibrated structural forecast rather than ranking on features.

## Accounting correction, adopted 2026-11-13

The stored hedged hold H unwinds the whole stock hedge at expiry and also charges 5 bp to dispose
of the assigned share. Under physical assignment that share nets against the hedge, so only the
residual `h - sign(S - K)` trades. Lane C found it (trial 1,856) and lane A reproduced it
independently on the full blocks, rebuilding the stored last hedge exactly: +0.22% of m0 per trade
[+0.21, +0.23] on 2013-18 and 2019-24, taking H to -0.82% [-1.27, -0.35] and -1.05%
[-1.45, -0.66]. Lane C's own +0.24% and +0.23% sit on its two-sided-quote universe with an
approximate hedge ratio; lane A's figures are the ones to quote. The gain is not uniform: netting
costs more than the old convention where the final hedge is small and the stock finishes in the
money, because the residual then trades at the equity half-spread rather than the flat 5 bp.

It moves comparisons, not only levels. The buy-back B has no expiry leg, so every H-against-B
figure moves by about +0.22%: lane A's perfect-foresight ceiling (shown above after netting), lane
F's resting buy-back, whose 1.74% deficit to the hold (2026, not recomputed) would widen by about
0.2% if the 2013-24 average carries over, and lane C's hold-or-close rule 1,851. Comparisons between hold variants move only by how often each finishes
in the money with a large residual. Lane B's strangle (1,826) is valued with a builder that
applies the old convention at a different rate, so part of its +0.55% is an accounting
difference not yet measured. The correction was not applied to the 2025-26 block.

The analyst signed it off on 2026-11-13. It is now in the package as
`HedgedHoldConfig.net_assignment` with the configuration `HEDGED_NET`, behind offline tests; the
shadow book marks with it. `HEDGED` keeps the old convention so that every study frozen before
this date reproduces its recorded numbers. `netting_reconciliation/reconcile.py` runs the closed
hedged-hold study's own event builder under both conventions: under `HEDGED` it reproduces the
stored H on all 7,753 events (largest gap 0.0), and under `HEDGED_NET` it gives +0.2187%
[+0.2096, +0.2292] of m0 on 2013-18 (N=2,640) and +0.2165% [+0.2068, +0.2278] on 2019-24
(N=5,113), equal to lane A's check to the fourth decimal. Netting costs more than the old
convention on 0.4% of hedged events; 961 events settle with no hedging session and are
unchanged.

## Whether the levers stack

The levers that held out act on different costs: the strike or wing choice on the entry spread
(B), the 15:45 entry clock on the entry spread (round 1, +0.50% of m0 on 2026H1), and the netting
correction on the expiry unwind. Adding point estimates to the 2019-24 hold brings it near zero,
but that sum is not a measurement: the two entry levers both save entry spread and overlap, and
they were measured on different blocks and contracts. A stacked book needs one frozen test, and
2019-24 is spent for contract choice and the hold exit. The forward paper book is the only clean
block left.

## Process failures, recorded

- Lane G's round-4 scorer printed the held-out blocks C and P beside discovery, spending trial
  1,977's held-out read, and the gap split behind the frozen trial 1,980 was glimpsed on every
  block during diagnosis. So 1,980's read on C was not clean either; it failed regardless.
- Lane J fitted winsorisation cut-offs and three pooled control betas on the full panel in rounds
  1 to 4; they carry no information on which names persist, and the held-out script refits on
  2019-24 only.
- Lanes A and C overlapped before agreeing a scope split: lane C's 1,841 and 1,843 repeat lane A's
  1,782 and 1,795, and lane A's HAR read on 2019-24 came six minutes before lane C's freeze on that
  block. Both lanes failed, so no verdict moves.
- Lane B's first null percentile for 1,820 was a NaN bug, corrected in a dated second row.
- Lane H's first two rounds (ten trials) were measurement defects, the second an outcome-dependent
  window search; both are logged as invalid or superseded.
- Freezes that fingerprint the note but not the scorer: lanes H and I hashed only the note; lane
  C's `r6_holdout_1851.py` was created 16 seconds after a freeze that called the scripts frozen
  "as they stand", lane H's `score_heldout.py` 21 seconds after its freeze, and lane J's
  `r5_heldout.py` was modified 28 seconds after its held-out log. Every one of those trials
  failed, so no verdict depends on it, but the freezes do not prove what they claim. Lane D's
  `heldout.py` was likewise not hashed, though its rule code was.
- Several levels are flattered by attrition and say so: lane F's 2026 tick coverage (covered B
  -1.41% against -6.32% uncovered) and lane C's two-sided-quote universe (dropped events H -6.92%).
