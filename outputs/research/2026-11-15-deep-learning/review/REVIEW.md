## Findings, ranked

**1. Blocks external use of the forecaster pass as a deep-learning result.** The +0.051 to +0.059 nats per event over L4 (trials 3,142, 3,145, 3,182) is a scale misspecification in L4 that three numbers repair. Exploratory reviewer check, chosen after the reads, not charged (charge from 3,212 if quoted): at each quarter start, one scale factor per tenor tercile for L4's own stored prior predictive, fitted by maximum log score on events expiring before the quarter, the same batch schedule as the networks. The recalibrated L4 gains +0.0482 [+0.0386, +0.0574] over L4 (N=5,113). Against it, D1 primary is +0.0030 [-0.0029, +0.0085], the D1b no-surface twin +0.0072 [+0.0007, +0.0139] and D2 +0.0110 [+0.0052, +0.0168]. The factors are stable from 2019 to 2024: 1.21 to 1.26 for short tenors (2 days or fewer), 0.93 to 0.95 for the middle tercile, and 0.70 to 0.75 for long tenors (over 15 days). Conservative sentence: "L4's error scale is constant across events and wrong by tenor; a flexible network adds at most about 0.01 nats per event beyond a three-parameter correction (exploratory)." Not L4's prior or burn-in: in 2019-24 L4 has at least 2,435 settled events at entry and a median predictive df of 976 to 1,821, and its scale varies by at most 1.7% across events within a year. Nor is it an artefact of batch refitting, because the three-parameter fix uses the same refits. The year profile (weak 2019 and 2020, strong from 2021) appears in the recalibrated L4 too. D1's explanation "as the training window grows" is therefore unsupported. The gain sits in the tenor tails: D2 gains +0.120, +0.018 and +0.104 by tercile. New ledger class: a benchmark with a misspecified error law makes any flexible challenger look informative.
